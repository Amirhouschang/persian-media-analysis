EVENTS
- 28 Dec 2025 – mid-Jan 2026: nationwide protests began over economic hardship and the rial's fall;
  an internet blackout began 8 Jan. Thousands were reported killed, but totals and breakdowns are disputed.
  Iranian authorities described violent participants as "rioters" and accused the USA and Israel of interference.
- 27 Jan: the rial reached about 1.5 million per US dollar; official year-on-year inflation about 60%.
- 6 Feb (Muscat), 17 Feb and 26 Feb (Geneva): Oman-mediated US–Iran nuclear talks; no agreement.
- 20 Feb: Trump set a 10-day deadline for a deal.
- 21–22 Feb: student protests at several universities, with some clashes with pro-government groups.
- 28 Feb: the USA and Israel launched large-scale strikes on Iran, killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and
  several senior officials and commanders. Iran retaliated against Israel and targets in Gulf states, including US bases,
  and sharply restricted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.
- From 28 Feb: Iran-aligned Iraqi militias (Islamic Resistance in Iraq, Kataib Hezbollah) attacked US forces
  and the US embassy in Baghdad; strikes hit these groups in Iraq.
- 28 Feb: a strike destroyed the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in Minab (Hormozgan), killing about 156 people,
  including about 120 children. Investigations by the UN, Amnesty International, Forensic Architecture and major media
  attribute it to a US missile strike; preliminary Pentagon findings pointed to outdated targeting data.
- 2 Mar: Hezbollah entered the war by firing on Israel; Israel intensified strikes in Lebanon and began ground operations on 16 Mar.
- 8 Mar: Iran's Assembly of Experts selected Mojtaba Khamenei as Supreme Leader.
- 18 Mar: Israeli strikes hit Iran's South Pars gas field, the first strike on Iran's upstream energy infrastructure.
- 31 Mar: Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa said Syria would stay out of the conflict unless attacked.
- 8 Apr: a Pakistan-mediated two-week US–Iran ceasefire took effect; 11–12 Apr Islamabad talks ended without agreement.
- 13 Apr: the USA imposed a naval blockade on Iranian shipping.
- 17 Apr: a 10-day Israel–Lebanon ceasefire took effect.
- 21 Apr: Trump unilaterally extended the US–Iran ceasefire while keeping the naval blockade in place.
- Throughout 2026: Iran executed several people convicted of spying for Israel (e.g. 22 Apr).
- 17–18 Jun: the US and Iran announced the "Islamabad Memorandum": a 60-day negotiating period,
  commercial passage through Hormuz, and a timetable for ending the US blockade; nuclear issues unresolved.
- 7–8 Jul: attacks on commercial vessels in/near Hormuz were followed by renewed US strikes and the collapse of the truce.
- 13–14 Jul: the USA reinstated the naval blockade.
- 20–22 Jul: Yemen's Houthis declared a maritime blockade on Saudi Arabia and claimed attacks on Saudi oil tankers.
- 20 Jul: Hamas named Khalil al-Hayya as its overall leader.
- 10 Aug: Mojtaba Khamenei reshuffled the military leadership (see KEY LIVING ACTORS).
- 24 Aug: Iran's new Persian Gulf Strait Authority blacklisted 45 tankers; the USA launched
  "Operation Economic Outcast" with new and secondary sanctions; the rial hit a record low of about 2 million per US dollar.
- Economy: official annual inflation stayed above 60% through the summer.
- Brent crude: about $71 before the war; futures high about $126 on 30 Apr; about $100 in late July; about $90 end of August.

INTERNATIONAL RELATIONS AND MEDIATORS
- Russia: Comprehensive Strategic Partnership treaty with Iran (signed 17 Jan 2025, in force 3 Oct 2025); no mutual
  defence clause, but neither side may support an aggressor against the other. In the 2026 war Russia condemned the
  US–Israeli strikes as violations of Iran's sovereignty and did not fight; US and European officials reported Russian
  intelligence and drone support. Russia profited from higher oil prices.
- Russia–Ukraine: the war continued throughout 2026 (short truces, e.g. 11–13 Apr); US-mediated talks produced no ceasefire.
  US–Russia relations were shaped by these talks, sanctions and US accusations of Russian support for Iran.
- China: Iran's largest oil buyer (imports fell sharply during the war); called for an immediate end of military
  operations, respect for Iran's sovereignty and reopening of Hormuz; Wang Yi met Araghchi in Beijing on 6 May.
  US officials reported deliveries of dual-use goods by Chinese firms.
- Pakistan: main mediator of the US–Iran ceasefire (8 Apr), the Islamabad talks and the Islamabad Memorandum (Jun).
- Qatar: long-standing mediator (e.g. Gaza 2025); hit by Iranian missiles and drones (Al Udeid base, Ras Laffan gas
  facility, Feb–Mar); officially did not take part in attacks on Iran and supported the Pakistan-led mediation.
- Oman: mediated the US–Iran nuclear talks before the war (Feb 2026).

INVOLVED STATES

- Jordan and Iraq host US forces and bases; attacks on these bases belong to the war.

INTERNATIONAL LAW – BASIC TERMS (neutral reference, applies equally to all states and actors)
- Sovereignty: every UN member state has the right to exist, to sovereignty and to territorial integrity
  (UN Charter Art. 2). The use of force against another state is prohibited, except in self-defence against
  an armed attack (Art. 51) or with authorisation of the UN Security Council.
- International humanitarian law (Geneva Conventions): parties to a conflict must distinguish between fighters
  and civilians. Attacks on civilians, children, hospitals, schools, homes and other civilian objects, and
  disproportionate attacks, are prohibited; attacks on nuclear power plants are specially restricted.
- War crimes: serious violations of these rules (Rome Statute of the International Criminal Court).
- Genocide: acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial or
  religious group (Genocide Convention 1948).
- Human rights (Universal Declaration of Human Rights; International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights,
  which Iran has ratified): right to life; prohibition of torture; no arbitrary arrest or detention;
  right to a fair trial and presumption of innocence; freedom of expression, assembly and religion;
  rights of ethnic, religious and linguistic minorities.
- These terms help to understand references in posts. They must not decide topic or tone on their own (rule 1).

GLOSSARY (Persian terms used in the posts)
- رهبر شهید / آقای شهید / امام شهید / امام خامنه‌ای: the late Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei (killed 28 Feb 2026).
- شهید (martyr): standard term for anyone killed on Iran's or the resistance side.
- جنگ تحمیلی ("imposed war"): state term for the 2026 war (historically also the 1980–88 Iran–Iraq war).
- وعده صادق ۴ ("True Promise 4"): name of Iran's missile and drone operations in the 2026 war.
- قرارگاه خاتم‌الانبیا: central headquarters of Iran's armed forces.
- محور مقاومت ("axis of resistance"): Iran-aligned groups (Hezbollah, Hamas, Iraqi militias, Houthis).
- اغتشاش / اغتشاشگران ("riots / rioters"): state terms for protests and protesters, esp. Dec 2025 – Jan 2026.
- سلطنت‌طلب: monarchists, opposition supporters of the former royal family.
- قرار شبانه / اجتماع شبانه / تجمع شبانه: nightly PRO-GOVERNMENT rallies in city squares during the war,
  in support of the armed forces and the system – not protests.
- جان‌فدا: pro-government campaign/parades during the war.
- کالابرگ: electronic food-coupon subsidy for households.
- اربعین: Shiite pilgrimage to Karbala (Iraq); دهه فجر: anniversary days of the 1979 revolution (1–11 Feb).
- Iran International, BBC Persian, Manoto: Persian-language media based outside Iran; the Iranian state calls them
  hostile and has labelled Iran International a terrorist organisation.
- Iranian calendar: year 1404 ends on 20 Mar 2026; 1405 begins on 21 Mar 2026.
