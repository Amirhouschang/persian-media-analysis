# Three Voices, One War: How Iranian News Channels Reported in 2026

[← back to overview](../../README.md)

Between 1 January and 31 August 2026, six Iranian news channels published 328,330 posts on Telegram. Three of them
speak for the state (IRNA, IRIB News, Mehr News), two are close to the Revolutionary Guards (Tasnim, Fars), one to the
reformist camp (Jamaran). These eight months include the protests at the turn of the year, the war between Israel, the
US and Iran from 28 February, a ceasefire and its collapse. This text summarises what the data show about that period.

**How it was analysed.** All posts were collected, cleaned in a database and counted in full: terms, names and
labels, per week and per group. Which terms a group uses clearly more often than the others was determined
statistically. In addition, a locally run language model assigned 10,750 posts to topics; on 200 new, hand-checked
posts, the topic matched in 72.5% of cases. All figures, charts and limitations are in
[04 – Analysis](04_analysis.md).

## January and February: protests, internet shutdown, negotiations

The protests over prices and the collapse of the currency had begun at the end of December. In the first week of
January, opponent labels in the IRGC-affiliated channels reach their peak: "rioters", "agitators",
"counter-revolution". From 8 January the internet is shut down. In the following week the state channels, too,
portray the protests as terrorism directed from abroad – with terms such as "terrorists", "armed" and "Mossad".
That week Jamaran publishes only 20 posts a day, IRNA none at all.

At the same time the data show how the channels see the outside world. The capture of Venezuela's president Maduro by
the US on 3 January is called an "abduction" and a breach of international law. When the EU agrees politically on
29 January to list the Revolutionary Guards as a terrorist organisation, the channels call it "hostile" and
"irresponsible". In February the nuclear talks with the US in Muscat and Geneva dominate; in the week of Muscat,
Jamaran writes almost twice as much about them as the others.

## From 28 February: the war displaces everything

With the attacks by the US and Israel and the death of Ali Khamenei, everything changes at once. The channels post
about three times as much as before, Mehr News more than 500 posts a day. Before the war, 4–5% of posts were military;
now it is 36–45%; domestic politics falls from about a quarter to under a tenth. On 1 March, the day of the official
confirmation, all six channels write "the martyr-leader" for the first time. Terms for crimes reach their peak in the
first week of the war – the week of the attack on the primary school in Minab, with about 156 dead.

On 8 March Mojtaba Khamenei is elected successor. No channel calls him "leader" before – all six do so for the first
time on that day. Even so, the dead man remains more present than the living one: after the election, about three
quarters of all mentions of the leader refer to Ali Khamenei.

The channels' world shrinks to the region. Russia and China are mentioned only a third as often in the
IRGC-affiliated channels, Gaza only a fifth to a third as often. Bahrain and Kuwait, which played no role before,
appear: Iran attacks US bases there. President Pezeshkian also recedes – in the IRGC-affiliated channels he drops to
less than a third.

## April to June: ceasefire and mediation

On 8 April a ceasefire takes effect, mediated by Pakistan. Pakistan is now mentioned in all six channels more than in
any other phase. The IRGC-affiliated channels report from Islamabad with their own correspondents, and with them
parliament speaker Ghalibaf, who negotiates there, comes into view: in the week from 13 April he is mentioned about
three times as often there as on average. That week the US imposes a naval blockade, and "Trump" reaches its highest
value in all groups.

During the ceasefire, Lebanon is the most-mentioned country; in early June, during the escalation around south
Beirut, it reaches the highest weekly value of all countries. The Gulf states show how differently the groups see
the same country: for the state media the United Arab Emirates are a currency-exchange hub, for the IRGC-affiliated
channels a target of attack (the port of Fujairah, early May), for Jamaran an actor in US politics. A week after
Fujairah the topic is reports of a secret visit by Netanyahu to the Emirates. China reaches its highest value not
through an event involving Iran but through Trump's trip to Beijing in mid-May.

In these weeks Jamaran is the only channel to make the internet a topic – five times as often as the others, with the
debate over separate access ("Internet Pro") and reopening. On 17 June the "Islamabad Memorandum" is signed.

## July: funeral, collapse, escalation in the Gulf

The highest values for "martyr" and "revenge" come not at the start of the war but four months later. From 3 to
10 July the funeral ceremony and funeral processions for Ali Khamenei take place – in Tehran, Qom, Najaf, Karbala and
Mashhad. In this period the IRGC-affiliated channels write "martyr" 21.6 times per 1,000 words, ten times as often as
usual, and "blood revenge" more often than in the first week of the war. Even Jamaran writes "Zionist regime" instead of
"Israel" 44% of the time that week – more often than at any other point.

On 7 and 8 July the ceasefire collapses. Afterwards the state and IRGC-affiliated channels call Israel the "Zionist
regime" in 57% and 53% of cases, more than ever. In July they report on "Operation Thunderbolt", attacks on US bases
in Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan. Saudi Arabia is now mentioned four and a half times as often in the IRGC-affiliated
channels as before – as an adversary in Yemen, where the Houthis impose a naval blockade on the country from 20 July.
Hezbollah, constantly present during war and ceasefire, falls back almost to its pre-war level: the focus shifts to
the Gulf, Iraq and Yemen.

## Three voices

Over the whole period the three groups speak distinctly differently.

**The state channels** speak as government and administration: foreign ministry spokesmen, ministers, provincial
authorities – and in the language of international law, with "aggression", "condemnation" and "United Nations".
Outside the war, a large part of their posts consists of service announcements.

**The IRGC-affiliated channels** report from the scene: missiles, drones, impacts, arrests, "unrest". They mention
Hezbollah almost twice as often as the others, mobilise with "oath of allegiance" and "revenge", and reach about ten
times as many views per post.

**Jamaran** is the channel of diplomacy – with the highest share of this topic in every phase. It sees almost every
country through the lens of the US, writes "Trump claims" almost three times as often as the others, and mostly says
"Israel". Former presidents and ministers of the reformist camp – Khatami, Rouhani, Zarif – appear almost only here:
Khatami 366 times, in all three state channels together 122 times.

## What the data do not show

Counting is not understanding: a term counts the same whether it is used approvingly, at a distance or as a quotation.
Assigning weeks to events is an interpretation based on the data. The AI assigns the topic correctly in about three of
four cases; it does not recognise tone reliably enough to compare groups – tone is therefore not evaluated. And the
reformist camp is represented by only one channel.

## Conclusion

The war did not make the three voices alike. It directed all channels to the same events, but each group tells them in
its own way: the state channels as administration and international law, the IRGC-affiliated channels as front and
mobilisation, Jamaran as negotiation and world politics. The channels differ most clearly not in the first shock of the
war but in the mourning for the killed leader in July – and in the language that grows harsher afterwards.
